Portfolio Part II. Hand Drawn Data Visualisation

A Week in My Life

About this piece

Part II of the portfolio was a personal data visualization based on Dear Data (Lupi & Posavec, 2016) and the 19th century statistical graphics by William Playfair (1786) and Florence Nightingale (1858). For this assignment, I chose to design my own visualisation from personal data, a typical week of my life, reconstructed from memory rather than tracked live.

The visualization consists of seven columns that represent one day per column. Each column represents a day from Monday to Sunday at the scale of 1cm per hour from midnight to midnight. The two variables used in this visualisation are time (represented through the length of each block) and type of activity (represented through colours and textures). My study cluster, including self study and classes, has a very intense colour, while football and gym have a different colour and all other activities (such as sleep, eat, travel, relax and errands) are represented in neutral grey color. This visualization was intended to use the principle that position and length are more easily estimated than colour and area (Cleveland & McGill, 1984). In other words, the main point of this piece is that 'this is a life balanced in a quality way, not just a busy one'.

Reflection

The best aspect of this visualization is the encoding itself. The passage of time is conveyed by the length of the blocks while activities are encoded by color, all of which is measured accurately at the rate of one centimeter for every hour. Precision is important. An aesthetically pleasing hand drawn graph with incorrect proportions is no data visualization. There were some real design decisions that I made in addition to following my initial visualization idea.

If I had more time, I would have fully embraced the historical aesthetic rather than seeing it as the last stage of my process. A handwritten title and a hand drawn border take more time to perfect than I initially anticipated. I would have spent more time working on the color and shading stage as well, considering that the majority of the visual elements rely on this stage.

The greatest discovery I made during the process was that color and symbols in this graphic represent two different narratives rather than compete with each other for attention. Red blocks show how I spend my time, and studying clearly wins the competition. Symbols like family phone calls, playing football, going to church appear irrespective of how busy my day might be. Without both, my graphic would not have been a truthful representation of my week.
